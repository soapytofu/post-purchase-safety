# SafeKeep — retailer discovery and design-partner guide

October 10, 2026 · Use with [the business-pilot spec](BUSINESS_PILOT_SPEC.md).

## Purpose

Learn how retailers actually identify and respond to affected inventory. Validate pain, data access, buyer authority and willingness to change before pitching a finished solution.

Initial hypothesis: independent U.S. baby-and-children’s retailers. Target 10–15 interviews across owners/operations leads and store managers; include businesses with established tools, not just enthusiastic friends. Record business size, product scope and interview role so unlike workflows are not averaged together.

Do not collect customer lists, purchase histories, credentials or unredacted supplier-confidential documents. Ask permission before taking notes or recording; recording is optional. Keep identifying contact details separate from research summaries. Do not upload partner documents into an AI service without explicit permission and agreed handling.

## Outreach draft

Subject: How does your store handle product recalls?

Hi [Name],

I’m researching how independent baby-and-children’s retailers check product recalls against inventory and coordinate store responses. I’m building SafeKeep, an early product in this area, but I’d first like to understand your current process—not sell you a finished system.

Would you be open to a 30-minute conversation about a recent recall or safety notice? No customer information or system access is needed. If useful, you could walk through a redacted example.

Thanks,
[Your name]

## 30-minute interview

### 0–3 minutes: permission and context

“I’m testing an idea, so examples that show you don’t need it are valuable too. Is it okay if I take notes? Please don’t share confidential or customer information.”

1. What is your role, and what kinds of products/locations do you manage?
2. Who owns product-safety notices and inventory decisions? Who would approve a new tool?

### 3–13 minutes: reconstruct an actual event

3. Tell me about the most recent recall or safety notice that might have affected your stock. When was it?
4. How did you first hear about it? What did you do next?
5. Can you walk me through how you decided which products or units were affected?
6. What did you need to check on a package, in inventory software, or with a supplier?
7. Who removed or checked stock, and how did you know the work was complete across locations?
8. Where did you record what happened? What remained uncertain?

Follow up with: “What happened then?”, “Can you give an example?”, “How long did that step take?”, “Was that typical?” Distinguish time estimates from demonstrated records. Do not imply they missed a recall or breached a rule.

If there was no recent event, ask about the last supplier withdrawal, product hold or practice drill. Mark it as a different event type, not evidence of recall frequency.

### 13–21 minutes: current tools and data reality

9. What works well in your process? What, if anything, is frustrating enough to change?
10. Which supplier emails, trade services, inventory/POS systems or recall tools do you already use? Why were they chosen?
11. Does your inventory distinguish store SKU, barcode, manufacturer/model, lot and location? Which fields are actually populated?
12. How often are quantities updated, and can you export them? What does a redacted export look like?
13. What happens when a notice identifies only particular lots, production dates or serial numbers?
14. What would prevent you from sharing a limited inventory export with a new service?

Ask about permissions, cost, staff effort, contractual restrictions and unsupported product types. Do not assume a barcode exists or that a supplier SKU is a universal identifier.

### 21–26 minutes: show the concept only now

“The proposed pilot would take a CSV inventory export, surface potential matches to available official notices, and help your team document review and removal. It would show missing data and source limitations. It would not certify safety, replace your inventory system or automatically stop sales.”

15. Where, if anywhere, would this fit into the event you just described?
16. What would be redundant? What would you still have to do outside it?
17. What evidence would you need before trusting an alert? Which mistakes would make you stop using it?
18. Who would use it, and who would maintain the inventory export?

Avoid “Wouldn’t this save time?” or “Would you use an AI recall platform?” Ask participants to explain concrete changes to their existing work.

### 26–30 minutes: commitment and next step

19. What would need to be true for you to try this with one location?
20. What budget/process would apply to a tool like this? What do existing approaches cost in money or staff time?
21. If appropriate: “We’re testing a $100–$300/month hypothesis for a small, assisted pilot after the workflow is proven. How would you evaluate that expense, and who decides?” Record the exact price discussed; do not treat a hypothetical yes as a purchase.
22. Would you be willing to review a mock case or provide an approved, redacted inventory sample? Agree a specific next step/date only if they volunteer a commitment.

## Synthesis after every five interviews

Compare participants by segment and role. Count actual examples, not just opinions:

- Recent relevant workflow pain demonstrated.
- Current tools already solve the problem adequately.
- Usable, permissioned inventory export available.
- Someone accepts responsibility for maintaining fresh inventory.
- Named buyer and plausible budget/process.
- Specific pilot commitment versus general enthusiasm.

Write a one-paragraph decision: retain the wedge, narrow it, change it, or stop. List the strongest disconfirming evidence and the next question to test. Do not manufacture a market-size estimate from this small sample.

## Design-partner agreement checklist

Before real-data onboarding, agree in writing:

- Sponsor, daily user, one/two pilot locations and supported product categories.
- Four-week proposed duration, drill schedule and check-in cadence.
- Exactly which data is shared, ownership, access, retention, deletion and permitted processing.
- Inventory refresh owner/cadence; failure and stale-data handling.
- No complete-coverage, emergency-support, safety-certification or compliance guarantee.
- Continued use of existing recall/supplier channels and independent verification.
- Founder-assisted versus self-service work; expected support hours and response availability.
- Price, if any, explicit approval and end-of-pilot continuation/cancellation process.
- No logos, testimonials or public case study without separate permission.
- Outcome metrics, known limitations and a named contact for concerns.

Interviews and outreach require founder authorization; this guide does not send messages or enroll anyone.
